Area Research

High-level, public notes on Gran Canaria to help us pick where to spend the scouting trip. Nothing here is a decision — just orientation.

Calendar Area Research

Climate, in one line

Mild and dry most of the year, with the north (around Las Palmas) cooler and greener and the south sunnier and more resort-oriented. Trade winds keep summers comfortable. Good for someone escaping long, dark winters.

North vs. South (general feel)

  • Las Palmas (north): the island’s main city — a real working city with a beach, schools, hospitals, and year-round life. Less “holiday”, more “live here”.
  • South coast: warmer and sunnier, built around tourism. Easy for a first soft landing, but quieter for actual daily family life out of season.
  • Inland / mountains: dramatic and cheaper, but car-dependent and far from services. Probably not for a first year.

Things to verify on the ground

  • School options and language of instruction near each shortlisted area.
  • Realistic commute / walkability without owning two cars.
  • Internet quality for remote work (ask to test it, don’t trust the brochure).
  • How the neighborhood feels mid-week in low season, not just on a sunny Saturday.

Getting there

One main airport on the island with frequent connections to mainland Spain and northern Europe. Worth checking seasonal direct routes before assuming travel home is easy.

Open questions

Residence/visa category and its document list; healthcare for the first months; whether we rent long or buy later. All of these go in the vault, not on this page.
