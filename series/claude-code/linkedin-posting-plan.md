# Run the Docs — Claude Code — LinkedIn Posting Plan

## Cadence
Daily (5x/week, weekdays) is ABOVE the proven band. Research supports 2-5x/week for sustained reach; daily sits at the top edge and only works if every post clears the quality bar — a weak post drags the next ones down because the algorithm reads recent engagement as a signal. Recommendation: commit to daily for week 1 as a launch sprint (a 5-episode series benefits from momentum and "see you tomorrow" continuity), but watch two metrics. If average impressions or engagement rate drop materially across Days 3-5 versus Days 1-2, fall back to a proven 3-4x/week rhythm (e.g., Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri, skipping Wednesday). Do NOT sacrifice video quality or hook sharpness to hit a daily quota — one strong post beats two mediocre ones. Treat daily as the ceiling, not the floor.

## Best time
Audience is developers + technical decision-makers; you (Invotek) and a large share of the target are in CET (Oslo). Post Tue-Thu mornings for peak B2B reach, roughly 08:00-10:00 CET (catches the Europe workday start and US East Coast early risers). Mon and Fri skew lower on LinkedIn, so put your two strongest episodes (Ep1 first session, Ep2 agentic loop) on Tue/Wed where reach is best, and use Mon/Fri for the lighter-lift episodes. Concretely: aim for ~09:00 CET on weekdays. Post, then stay active for the first 60-90 minutes replying to every comment — early engagement velocity is what the feed rewards. Avoid posting and walking away; the first hour matters more than the exact minute.

## First-comment strategy
Post the first comment yourself within 1-2 minutes of publishing, from the same author account. Put everything link-y and "next step" here so the post body stays link-free (external links in the body suppress reach). Structure of the first comment: (1) the one link you want clicked — series playlist, the docs page, or the Invotek "work with us" page — with a one-line reason to click; (2) a continuity hook for the series ("Tomorrow: Ep[N+1] — [teaser]. Follow Invotek so it lands in your feed."); (3) a re-ask of the post's question to lower the friction to reply, since some people comment on the comment. After Day 1, also pin or reply-link to the previous episode so the series compounds (each post drives discovery of the others). Keep the comment to ~3 short lines. Then spend the first 60-90 minutes replying to every comment with a real follow-up question, not just "thanks" — each reply is fresh engagement the algorithm counts.

## Tips
- Lead with the outcome, never the feature. 'Ship a fix in seconds' beats 'Claude Code has an agentic loop.' Views don't pay; results attract B2B clients — every post should hint at what a team gains.
- Write the first line last and stress-test it: cover everything but line 1 and ask 'would a busy dev stop scrolling for this?' If not, rewrite. The hook does ~80% of the reach work.
- Burn captions into the video. Autoplay is muted, so a silent viewer must follow the whole thing. Vertical-ish (4:5 or 9:16) wins more screen real estate in the feed than 16:9.
- Keep each episode to 45-75 seconds. Show one concrete thing working on a real (even if small) task. Demonstrate the outcome on screen — don't just narrate it.
- No external link in the post body. Put the link in the first comment, posted by you within 2 minutes. This is the single biggest avoidable reach killer.
- Reply to every comment in the first 90 minutes, and reply with a question to keep threads alive. Comment volume and reply velocity weigh heavily — guarding the first hour beats posting at the 'perfect' minute.
- Build series continuity: end each post and first comment with a one-line teaser for tomorrow's episode and a 'follow Invotek' nudge. A 5-part arc gives people a reason to come back.
- End every post with a paired CTA + question. The CTA gives a next action ('try it on your next task'); the question gives a low-effort way to comment ('what would you automate first?').
- Watch the numbers across Days 1-5. If impressions/engagement sag on Days 3-5, that's your signal that daily is too hot for now — drop to 3-4x/week (e.g., skip Wednesday) without apology. Cadence serves reach, not the other way around.
- Stay on-brand: practical, no hype, no emojis in the copy. For a dev consultancy, credibility is the conversion lever — sounding like a marketer undercuts the authority you're building.
- Repurpose: the same 5 videos become a single carousel recap or a 'Week 1 of Claude Code, in 5 clips' post for the following Monday — extends the life of the work and re-surfaces the series to people who missed it.

### Ep 5
**Hook:** Your best prompts shouldn't live in one person's head.

Most teams retype the same instructions every day.

Claude Code fixes that with slash commands.

Type / for built-ins:
- /review — review the current diff
- /resume — pick up where you left off
- /clear — reset the context

Then save your own. Drop a markdown file in .claude/commands/, add $ARGUMENTS for inputs, commit it.

Now your whole team runs the same prompt, the same way — straight from git. 30 seconds in the demo.

**CTA:** Watch the 30s terminal demo, then steal the pattern. What's the one prompt your team retypes every single day?
